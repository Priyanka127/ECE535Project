What was added
- 861 distinct numerical recordings from 8 participants, 27 actions, and 20 skeleton joints.
- Joints include head, shoulders, elbows, wrists, hands, spine, pelvis, hips, knees, ankles and feet.
- These are 3D coordinate sequences. RGB videos, depth images and IMU data are separate downloads; they were not needed or downloaded for this study.
- MATLAB variable d_skel has shape 20 joints x 3 coordinates x number of frames.
- Actual archive inspection found 58,299 frames; recordings contain 41 to 125 frames.
- The 3 absent recordings are a8_s1_t4, a23_s6_t4 and a27_s8_t4.
- Archive SHA256: f029ea1d4e4c330b2c61307b6d532b34c3920bcac1f4c22c7827f406e2c7ac00

The filename is retained so the existing Colab link remains valid; the notebook title now includes head and full-body movement.

What the experiment asks
Can full-body skeleton measurements recognize one of eight previously enrolled participants in a different recording?
The primary split trains on trials 1-3 and tests on trial 4. Entire recordings stay together.
A separate exploratory split trains on actions 1-21 and tests on actions 22-27.
Head-relative, full-body pose/movement and full-body movement-only features use identical participants and splits.
Chance is 12.5% among eight identities. These scores are recording-level, not five-second-window-level.

Important interpretation
This dataset was designed for action recognition using a fixed laboratory Kinect camera, not for headset-based privacy research. We use it for a new, limited skeleton-identification pilot.
Pose and body proportions can carry identity information. A post-hoc bone-length diagnostic is included to avoid attributing all accuracy to movement style.
Do not combine these participants with the Roma Tre people. Do not compare raw percentages across the two datasets as evidence that full-body sensing is superior: the cohorts, sensors, actions, participant counts and prediction units differ.
No different-day, different-device or unseen-person performance has been established. The exploratory test set should not be reused for tuning and then presented as untouched validation.
Coordinate units are not asserted from the release documentation. Features are centered on the pelvis and normalized by torso length; movement changes are measured per frame. The nominal source capture rate is approximately 30 fps.

Access and attribution
The original paper describes a public-domain release and IRB-approved consent; the landing page does not assign a named Creative Commons license. Preserve the source and attribution. No account registration or license-signing was required for the skeleton archive.
Cite: C. Chen, R. Jafari, and N. Kehtarnavaz, "UTD-MHAD: A Multimodal Dataset for Human Action Recognition Utilizing a Depth Camera and a Wearable Inertial Sensor," IEEE International Conference on Image Processing (ICIP), 2015.

Why this dataset
Its repeated participant-labelled trials, simple skeleton format and small separate modality download make a practical addition. EgoBody is a more direct headset-related option but requires registration, a signed license and more preparation. CMU mocap warns that one person may have multiple subject IDs, complicating identification evaluation.
